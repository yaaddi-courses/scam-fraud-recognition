# Cloned voices and the codes that protect you

**Covers:** Voice cloning, AI, PIN

## When a voice is no longer proof {#overview}
For most of history, if you heard a familiar voice on the phone, you knew who was calling. That used to be a safe assumption, and today it is not. Software can copy a voice from a short recording, and it does it cheaply. Scammers use this to make a call that seems to come from "a relative in trouble". So we need a new habit: check the person in a second way, and guard the secret codes that prove who you are.

## AI {#ai}
You have probably heard "AI" so often that it has lost its meaning, so let's pin it down. AI, or artificial intelligence, is software that does jobs that normally take human thinking, such as recognizing speech, writing text or making images. Modern systems learn from huge numbers of examples instead of following rules a person wrote by hand.

AI is not good or bad by itself. It helps with translation, medical images and spam filters. But the same abilities make fakes cheaper and more convincing: a letter with perfect grammar, a photo of a person who does not exist, a copied voice. A scam no longer needs a skilled criminal. It needs a tool and a few minutes.

## Voice cloning {#voice-cloning}
Here is how it works. Voice cloning means using AI to recreate someone's voice from a short sample, and then making it say new sentences. The sample can come from a social media video, a voicemail greeting or a recorded talk. A few seconds to a minute of clear speech is often enough for a rough copy.

A typical scam is a call that sounds like a grandchild or a boss: "I have been in an accident and I need money now. Please don't tell anyone." The voice may even sound upset. Notice what surrounds the voice: urgency, secrecy and a request for fast payment. Those are the real giveaways, and they are the same in every version of this scam.

What can you do? Hang up and call the person back on the number you already have. Ask a question only they could answer. Agree on a family safe word ahead of time and use it whenever money is requested. And think about how much of your voice you post in public.

## PIN {#pin}
A PIN, a personal identification number, is a short secret code that shows you own a bank card or account. Because it is short, it is easy to remember, and just as easy to lose if you hand it over.

Here is a rule worth keeping: banks and card companies never ask for your full PIN by phone, text or email. Anyone who does is not from your bank, however convincing the voice. Never share it with a caller, even one who says they are the police. Avoid obvious numbers such as a birth year, and do not use the same PIN for several cards.

## A worked example {#example}
Let's walk through a call together. It is 11 p.m., the number is unknown, and the voice sounds exactly like your nephew.

1. The voice says he has been arrested and needs 2,000 dollars wired today. He begs you not to tell his parents.
2. You spot the three warning signs: urgency, secrecy and a payment.
3. You say, "I will call you right back," and you hang up.
4. You dial his real number from your contacts. He answers, safe at home.
5. You report the number and tell your family about the scam.

If you cannot reach him, ask for the family safe word on a second call, or contact his parents.

Compare the costs. The check takes two minutes. Sending the money cannot be undone.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is thinking, "I know his voice too well to be fooled." Cloned voices fool people who have known the speaker for years, especially when the caller is crying or in a hurry. Judge the situation, not the sound. Another slip is giving out your PIN to "prove who you are". No bank needs it for that.

## Key points {#key-points}
- AI can copy a voice from a few seconds of audio, so a familiar voice no longer proves identity.
- Hang up and call back on a number you already have, and agree on a family safe word.
- Urgency, secrecy and a payment request are the signs of this scam.
- Never share your PIN with anyone who calls, texts or emails you.
