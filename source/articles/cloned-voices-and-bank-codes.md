# Cloned voices and the codes that protect you

**Covers:** Voice cloning, AI, PIN

## When a voice is no longer proof {#overview}
For most of history, recognizing someone's voice was a reliable way to know who you were talking to. That is no longer true. Software can now copy a voice from a short recording, and it can do it cheaply. Scammers use this to make a call from "a relative in trouble". Defending against it needs a change of habit: you check the person in another way, and you guard the secret codes that prove who you are.

## AI {#ai}
AI, or artificial intelligence, is software that does tasks that normally need human thinking, such as recognizing speech, writing text or making images. Modern systems learn from large amounts of examples instead of following rules written by hand.

AI is neither good nor bad in itself. It helps with translation, medical images and spam filters. The same abilities make fakes cheaper and more convincing: a letter written with perfect grammar, a photo of a person who does not exist, or a copied voice. A scam no longer needs a skilled criminal. It needs only a tool and a few minutes.

## Voice cloning {#voice-cloning}
Voice cloning is using AI to recreate a person's voice from a short sample and then speaking new sentences in it. The sample can come from a social media video, a voicemail greeting or a recorded talk. A few seconds to a minute of clear speech is often enough for a rough copy.

A typical scam is a call that sounds like a grandchild or a boss: "I have been in an accident and need money now", "Please do not tell anyone". The voice may even sound upset. The tricks around it are the usual ones: urgency, secrecy and a request for a fast payment.

Defences are practical. Hang up and call the person back on the number you already have. Ask a question that only they could answer. Agree on a family safe word ahead of time and use it whenever money is requested. Be careful with how much of your voice you post publicly.

## PIN {#pin}
A PIN is a personal identification number, a short secret code that proves you are the owner of a bank card or account. Because it is short, a PIN is easy to remember and also easy to steal if you give it away.

Banks and card companies will never ask you for your full PIN by phone, text or email. A person who asks for it is not from your bank, no matter how convincing the voice. Never share it with a caller, including one who claims to be the police. Do not use obvious numbers such as a birth year, and do not reuse the same PIN for different cards.

## A worked example {#example}
You get a call at 11 p.m. from a number you do not know. The voice sounds exactly like your nephew.

1. The voice says he has been arrested and needs 2,000 dollars wired today. He begs you not to tell his parents.
2. You notice the three warning signs: urgency, secrecy and a payment.
3. You say: "I will call you right back." You hang up.
4. You dial his real number from your contacts. He answers, safe at home.
5. You report the number and tell the family about the scam.

If you cannot reach him, ask for the family safe word on a second call, or contact his parents.

Compare the costs. The check takes two minutes. Sending the money cannot be undone.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Believing that you could never be fooled because you know the voice well. Cloned voices fool people who know the speaker for years, especially when the caller is crying or in a hurry. Judge the situation, not the sound. Another mistake is giving your PIN to "verify" your identity: no bank needs it for that.

## Key points {#key-points}
- AI can copy a voice from a few seconds of audio, so a familiar voice no longer proves identity.
- Hang up and call back on a number you already have, and agree on a family safe word.
- Urgency, secrecy and a payment request are the signs of this scam.
- Never share your PIN with anyone who calls, texts or emails you.
